Case 001 · Jvara

Persistent Jvara with Kasa

Low-grade fever for 5 days, worse in the evenings, with a dry cough and mild throat irritation. Appetite (Agni) is reduced. Prakriti is Kapha-Pitta.

Jvara (Fever)Kasa (Cough)Kapha-Pitta (Constitution)Aruchi (Loss of Appetite)
Your Proposed Formulation
✦ Reference Answer

Vyaghryadi Kashaya, combined with Tribhuvankirti Rasa for the febrile component, is preferred here because the presentation is a localized respiratory Jvara-Kasa rather than a systemic inflammatory pattern — the evening-predominant low grade fever and dry cough point to Kapha-Avarana of Pittaja Jvara.

— Bhaishajya Ratnavali, Jvara Chikitsa Prakarana; cross-referenced in Yogaratnakara.

Case 002 · Agnimandya

Agnimandya with Bloating

Weak digestive fire, post-meal heaviness and bloating (Adhmana), irregular bowel habit, coating on the tongue. Vata-Kapha prakriti, sedentary occupation.

Agnimandya (Weak Digestion)Adhmana (Bloating)Vata-Kapha (Constitution)
Your Proposed Formulation
✦ Reference Answer

Hingvashtak Churna is the classical first line for Vata-predominant Agnimandya with Adhmana; Chitrakadi Vati is a suitable alternative where Kapha-Avarana of Agni dominates. Avipattikar is reserved for Pitta-type digestive complaints, which this case does not show.

— Sharangdhara Samhita, Madhyama Khanda.

Case 003 · Kushtha

Chronic Kushtha, Pitta-Kapha

Recurring, itchy, discolored skin patches for over a year, worse with dairy and jaggery. Mild oozing on scratching. Pitta-Kapha prakriti, diabetic history in the family.

Kushtha (Skin Disorder)Kandu (Itching)Pitta-Kapha (Constitution)
Your Proposed Formulation
✦ Reference Answer

Panchatikta Ghrita Guggulu paired with Gandhaka Rasayana is favoured for chronic Kushtha with Kandu. Note the safety flag: given the family history of diabetes, any anupana or adjunct containing jaggery should be avoided and replaced with warm water.

— Charaka Samhita, Chikitsa Sthana, Kushtha Chikitsa.
